# Landscape and Greek road edging

Camp streets and the eleven land-based temple approaches are paved in simple warm-grey limestone flagstones. Slabs have irregular outlines, narrow earth-coloured joints, subtle surface grain and softly bevelled edges. A shared material uses world-space coordinates so slab sizes stay consistent along bends and across junctions. The existing Greek-key kerbs remain. Road surfaces follow the terrain with no raised collision steps. Forest trails remain earth paths. The older offline Blender study below predates this paving and does not depict the final road material.

The meadow's icosahedron flower proxies, the solid garden-bed flower clumps, and the old solid shapes in the terraced gardens and bench pots are replaced by three modelled varieties: cream daisies, muted rose poppies and lavender. Each shared model has three complete curved stems, folded leaves and individual flower heads. Petals have curvature, and daisy/poppy centres include pollen detail. Flowers are planted at sampled terrain height; pots use their actual raised soil level. Spatial instance batches share three geometries and one material, with gentle stem-weighted wind. The existing trees, dense forest floor and blocky weathered rocks remain.

The previous road edging had a 650-instance limit and stopped before reaching the entire path network. The replacement covers all twelve camp routes and eleven sandy temple approaches. Chamfered limestone blocks with a restrained Greek-key ribbon follow both sides at 1.04-metre arc-length intervals. Each block tilts with the terrain and is sunk slightly into it. Edging is omitted at junctions and overlaps so it cannot run across the walking route. Low blocks have no movement barrier. The four garden courts use matching blocks spaced around their elliptical borders, replacing their rectangular edging.

The terrain now adds detail locally at the lake bank, belvedere and small court knolls. Fine and coarse cells share intermediate vertices, avoiding gaps at their boundaries. Visual ground and collision use the same mesh. Grass and moss texture layers blend continuously instead of switching at one threshold. Garden soil meshes have radial subdivisions so the beds follow rolling ground rather than cutting across it as a single fan.

## Validation

`npm test` checks the full camp routes, twelve temple entrances, causeway, training ground, dock and lakeside access; it also covers road-border completeness, equal spacing, junction clearance, grounded placement, complete botanical geometry and closed terrain seams. Type checking, static build and fourteen portable-page checks are separate release checks.

`node scripts/preview-landscape.mjs` exports the runtime models for the offline Blender study. Run Blender with `--background --python scripts/blender/render_landscape.py -- <project-path>` to regenerate its two studies and editable `.blend` file in `assets-source/landmarks`. These renders inspect geometry, not browser shading: they omit the full camp architecture, vegetation textures, live mist and atmospheric shaders. Browser WebGL appearance and FPS remain unverified because local browser preview is unavailable. The existing hosted Sites project currently returns `project_not_found`, so this release is delivered through the portable package and GitHub source.
