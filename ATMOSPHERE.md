# Day and night atmosphere

The camp and both forest pages share a 32-minute cycle: daylight from 06:00 to 18:00 lasts 24 real minutes; night from 18:00 to the next 06:00 lasts 8 real minutes. A daylight game hour takes 120 real seconds and a night game hour takes 40 real seconds. The sun and moon remain continuous at the boundaries. A new clock starts at 08:12. The sun rises in the east (+X), arcs through the southern sky and sets in the west. The cratered moon rises in the east at sunset, passes through the southern sky at midnight and sets in the west at dawn, giving a full-moon night. Moon phases and seasons are not simulated. The clock uses a saved epoch and real elapsed time, so the sky continues through pauses, temple visits and page reloads when browser storage is available. Blocked storage falls back to memory. Local file URL storage sharing depends on the browser.

Three.js's atmospheric-scattering sky follows the moving sun and blends into a dark night gradient. Warm dawn/dusk colours transition into daylight, then cool lunar light. A deterministic field of 1,300 stars fades in at night and rotates with the sky. The moon has procedural maria, crater relief and limb shading. Bodies and stars follow the camera's translation without changing their angular direction. The HUD shows the current phase and game time.

Both celestial discs have a shared apparent diameter of 2.5 degrees, making them easier to read from the ground. The solar shader uses a soft disc edge; the moon uses a scaled unit sphere so its crater coordinates stay correct. Their enlargement is independent of orbit timing and illumination strength.

One directional light follows the active sun or moon. Its 4096-pixel shadow map updates at up to 10 Hz; body positions and illumination update every frame. The light fades to zero at the horizon before the other source takes over. This avoids two complete forest shadow passes. The camp and smaller expedition shadow projections are explicitly rebuilt after setting their extents. Ambient fill, fog, depth-aware forest mist and exposure follow the cycle. Warm point lights around the camp's existing lanterns fade on at dusk; they do not allocate extra shadow maps.

Night visibility uses stronger moonlight and ambient fill, brighter existing path lamps, and 42 additional bronze lanterns at the twelve temple entrances, Great Hall, Wayfarer Hall, dining/training areas, lakeside and approaches. Their visible glass glows at night. Eight shared point-light slots illuminate nearby lanterns within 65 metres of the camera, keeping the light count bounded; distant lanterns remain visible as emissive fixtures. The lights fade out in daylight. These small fixtures introduce no new movement barriers.

Four large cumulus banks replace the old small flattened cloud meshes. Each is a world-space volume: five overlapping lobes, two scales of 3D billow noise, height falloff and a 36-step optical-depth integration. Broad sunlight samples shade their interiors. Their slow, bounded drift starts at about 0.22 metres per second and has no wrap jump.

Soft cloud shadows use the same moving centres, sizes and lobe footprints, projected along the active sunlight or moonlight direction. Horizon projections fade out before division becomes unstable. Cloud shading changes colour and brightness through the cycle, including warm sunset light and cool dark night clouds. The effect attenuates directional light on terrain, plants, water and architecture; it does not darken point lights or emissive objects. Projected shadows approximate cloud columns rather than rendering another shadow map.

Mirror Lake remains opaque blue: `transparent=false`, `opacity=1`, `transmission=0`, `depthWrite=true`. It uses several wave-normal scales, grazing-angle sky/cloud reflections and moving solar/lunar highlights. Its reflection colours darken at night. Cloud reflections share the same bank positions and drift as the sky. There is no see-through lake surface.

`day-cycle.ts` shares time, celestial directions and lighting state with the atmosphere, water, HUD and renderer. `celestial-sky.ts` builds the moon and stars; `atmosphere-layout.ts` shares the cloud field. Tests sample the complete orbit and wrap boundary, daylight/night contrast, warm low sun, moon and star visibility, persisted/fallback clocks, dynamic lamp intensity, bounded shadow updates, actual projection extents, horizon safety and live lake/cloud uniform updates. Full game checks cover routes, landmarks and temple access. Runtime WebGL appearance and FPS remain unverified because browser preview access is unavailable. The existing hosted Sites project returns `project_not_found`; the updated source and portable game are delivered separately.
